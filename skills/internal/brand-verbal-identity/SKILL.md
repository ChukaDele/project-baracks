# Brand verbal identity

Words carry the brand more often than visuals do, especially in B2B services.

## Prerequisite

You need an approved positioning. If it is missing, use `brand-strategy` first.

## Step 1: Messaging house

Build one page with four levels.

1. **Core line.** What we do, for whom, and why it is different. One sentence. No adjectives that a competitor could also use.
2. **Point of view.** What we believe that the category gets wrong. This is where difference lives. Take a real stance, one that some buyers will reject.
3. **Three pillars.** Each pillar: claim, proof, and one buyer objection it answers.
4. **Proof stack.** Numbers, cases, named clients, artefacts, guarantees. No claim without proof at this level.

Kill any line that a direct competitor could publish unchanged. Test this by pasting it into a competitor's site in your head.

## Step 2: Conversion structure (StoryBrand, used carefully)

For landing and sales pages, order the page like this: buyer's problem, the stakes, our plan, the proof, the call to action, what failure looks like, what success looks like. The buyer is the hero.

Use SB7 for **structure only**. Do not use its stock phrasing. Thousands of sites use the same wording, so it removes distinctiveness while adding clarity. Keep the clarity; write the words fresh.

## Step 3: Voice and tone rules

Write rules a hired writer can follow with no meeting.

- Six voice traits, each with "we sound like this / not like this" pairs
- Ten words we use; ten words we never use
- Sentence rules: length, person, contractions, jargon level
- Tone shifts by situation: sales, onboarding, error, apology, bad news
- Three worked examples of the same message in our voice and in generic voice

## Step 4: Tagline territory

Do not write one tagline. Write three territories, each with three lines. State what each territory claims and what it gives up. Say plainly if no tagline is needed. Many strong service brands do not need one.

Neumeier's split is useful: a **tagline** is external and public. A **trueline** is internal and true, and it does not need to be pretty.

## Step 5: Naming the buyer's world

Write the words the buyer already uses for the problem. Use those words in headlines. Use your own invented terms only for things you own, and only after the buyer understands the offer.

## Output format

1. Messaging house (one page)
2. Point of view statement
3. Voice and tone guide with examples
4. Tagline territories with trade-offs
5. Homepage and sales page message map, section by section
6. Words we will never use
7. Killed messages and why

## Validation gates

- Could a competitor publish this line unchanged? If yes, rewrite.
- Does every claim have proof at pillar level?
- Would a buyer repeat the core line correctly after one read?
- Does the point of view risk anything? A stance no one can disagree with is not a stance.
- Is anything here only true because we want it to be?

## Limits

- Miller gives clarity, not distinctiveness.
- Jiwa gives emotional resonance, not proof.
- Voice does not fix a weak offer or a bad position.
