# Academic verify

Trigger only when the claim is consequential, contested, surprisingly precise, central to a decision, or likely to have changed. Do not run it for routine low-impact facts.

1. Normalize the exact claim, population, intervention/exposure, comparator, outcome and time.
2. Verify citation existence and identity independently of whether it supports the claim.
3. Read the paper and map the claimed proposition to methodology and actual result.
4. Judge separately: citation existence, directness of support, study-design strength, statistical strength, limitations and external validity.
5. Seek corrections, retractions, replications, systematic reviews and credible contradictions using provider-neutral retrieval.
6. State the current conclusion with calibrated uncertainty, freshness and citations. A real citation is not proof; statistical significance is not effect importance; one paper is not consensus.
