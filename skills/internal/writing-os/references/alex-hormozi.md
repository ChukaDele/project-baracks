# Alex Hormozi: business-writing and teaching reference

Use Hormozi for practical business, educational, framework, product, and launch writing. Do not make his preferences universal prose rules.

## Primary source set

1. **Be Unforgettable: How to Create Content That Resonates**, Matt Gray interview / The Game Ep. 669. Writing section covers simplicity, longevity, validity versus utility, narrow scope, exceptional products, and deliberate launch preparation.
2. **How I Wrote A #1 Bestseller with $0 and No Publisher**. Covers problem selection, story structure, framework design, editing, ideal-reader feedback, and book architecture.
3. **Alex Hormozi Teaches You How to Write a #1 Best Seller [9 TIPS]**. A compact list of sentence-level business-writing defaults.
4. **$100M Leads Launch** plus the behind-the-scenes launch episode. Covers preparation, compression, one idea per slide, pairing ideas with visuals, audience experience, and one-of-zero distinctiveness.

## What Hormozi owns

### 1. Solve a narrow problem completely

For practical nonfiction, a strong unit of work has a clear problem and aims to leave the reader able to act.

Ask:
- What exact problem is this piece solving?
- Can the scope be narrowed until the solution can be complete?
- Which section does not help solve that problem?
- What is the goal beyond the document itself?

Do not apply this to every literary or exploratory genre. An essay may explore rather than solve.

### 2. Simplicity protects cognitive capacity

The useful principle is not "third grade at all costs." It is that the reader should spend mental effort on the idea rather than translating inflated language.

Default for business and educational writing:
- ordinary words;
- active constructions;
- simple tense where natural;
- strong verbs instead of adverb-plus-weak-verb;
- short sentences when splitting does not damage the relationship between ideas;
- remove redundant words;
- define unfamiliar concepts in observable terms.

Exception:
Keep technical vocabulary when it is the precise term. Keep a longer sentence when the relationship between clauses is the point. Do not dumb down the idea.

### 3. Validity x utility

A framework can fail in two directions:
- so broad that it is nearly always true but tells the reader nothing useful;
- so specific that it works in one narrow case and breaks elsewhere.

For a proposed framework, ask:
- In how many contexts does this remain true?
- In those contexts, does it still tell the reader what to do?
- What counterexample breaks it?
- Can the framework be changed to absorb the counterexample without becoming empty?

This is a reasoning test, not a rhetorical test.

### 4. Define abstractions by behavior

When a definition feels circular or academic, describe what the thing causes people to do, what it changes, or how it can be observed.

This is especially useful for business abstractions such as brand, trust, value, accountability, leadership, and quality.

Do not reduce philosophical, legal, scientific, or technical definitions to behavior when that loses necessary meaning.

### 5. Story carries the experience behind a lesson

For teaching, a story can show where the lesson came from and why it mattered.

A practical story scaffold:
- setting;
- character;
- desire;
- struggle;
- turning insight;
- outcome;
- resolution or implication.

Include both external events and the internal experience only when the source supports them. Never invent internal states for a real person.

Use detail selectively. If a detail does not change the story, the reader's model, or the stakes, it may not belong.

### 6. Alternate load and relief

When conceptual material gets dense, a relevant story, example, diagram, or concrete case can reset the reader and provide context.

Do not insert anecdotes merely to entertain. The example must teach, test, or embody the point.

### 7. Feedback from target readers

For major practical work, test the draft with readers who resemble the intended audience.

Interpret feedback carefully:
- when several qualified readers independently identify the same problem, treat it as strong evidence of a defect;
- when feedback diverges, investigate rather than averaging preferences into blandness;
- the author retains responsibility for the final judgment.

### 8. Destructive editing

Useful Hormozi defaults for business prose:
- delete a sentence when the paragraph keeps the same meaning without it;
- prefer the shorter exact word to the longer equivalent;
- replace adverb-plus-weak-verb with a stronger verb when possible;
- split overloaded sentences when the clauses are independent;
- remove language that exists to sound intelligent.

The target is higher idea density, not minimum word count.

### 9. Exceptional work is a product-quality question

For high-investment artifacts such as books, flagship essays, keynote scripts, or public launches, ask whether the work itself deserves distribution.

A useful stopping test:
- Can the author name a concrete change that would make it better?
- If yes, make it or consciously reject it.
- If every proposed change merely makes it different or worse given current knowledge, ship.

Do not convert this into endless perfectionism. Narrow scope is the escape hatch: solve a smaller problem exceptionally well.

### 10. Preparation is part of writing

For high-stakes spoken or visual delivery:
- rehearse;
- record;
- review;
- revise wording and sequence;
- remove or add slides;
- pair one idea with one visual when useful;
- test the final medium rather than editing only in a document.

The higher the stakes and the less repeatable the event, the more preparation is justified.

### 11. One of zero

"Distinctive" is stronger when it is operational.

Ask:
- What can this writer say because of their actual evidence or experience?
- What can this piece show that generic competitors cannot?
- What work are we willing to do that most comparable writers will not?
- Does the unusual element improve value for the reader, or is it spectacle?

This complements Harry Dry's "can nobody else say this?" and the commodity-content test.

## Nine-rule short-form guide, correctly bounded

Hormozi's compact defaults are useful for business copy, ads, instructions, and clear exposition:
- present tense when natural;
- active voice;
- simple tenses;
- prefer strong verbs to adverbs;
- short sentences;
- accessible vocabulary;
- positive phrasing when it is clearer;
- remove redundancy;
- cut words that add no meaning.

These are defaults, not laws. The Writing Examples corpus demonstrates valid exceptions in literary, emotional, speech, and narrative work.

## Writing OS synthesis

Hormozi should make practical writing:
- easier to understand;
- harder to misunderstand;
- more useful;
- more specific;
- more complete within a narrow scope;
- more rigorously edited.

He should not make every genre sound like Alex Hormozi.
