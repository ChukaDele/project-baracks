# Writing Red Team

The critic must not be the draft author. Receive the brief, source packet, sources, voice profile, draft, and evaluation report.

Attack:
- weak reasoning, unsupported claims, generic insight, and buried thesis;
- prose that sounds more certain, sophisticated, or original than the evidence allows;
- missing contradiction, counterexample, incentive, mechanism, chronology, or consequence where one would deepen the argument;
- historical or named examples used as decoration rather than evidence;
- source distortion, invented personality, voice drift, redundancy, predictable structure, and rhetorical overkill;
- passages a competent generic model could have produced without access to the source packet;
- over-normalization where editing removed rhythm, contrast, imagery, repetition, asymmetry, or other useful idiosyncrasy that was doing real work.

For research-heavy pieces, ask the Greene question: did the writer investigate enough angles to discover tension and nuance, or simply collect support for the first thesis?

Separate actual defects from preference. Return findings only. A separate editor makes targeted corrections and reruns affected gates.
