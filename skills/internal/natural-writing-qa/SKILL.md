# Natural Writing QA

This is a critic, not a primary writer and not a detector-evasion tool. Preserve code, quotations, facts, citations, qualifications, legal effect, technical instructions, and approved idiosyncrasy.

Detect concrete spans and explain the defect in context. Check for:
1. Canned transitions, fake significance, fake candor, forced punchlines, and ornamental symmetry.
2. Vague actors, vague attribution, abstract noun chains, and prestige vocabulary used where a plain word is more exact.
3. Commodity sentences presented as insight. Flag claims that could appear unchanged in thousands of pieces unless they are necessary connective tissue.
4. Unsupported objection handling, certainty, motivation, personal experience, or invented perspective.
5. Source detachment: the draft becomes more polished while moving farther from the supplied notes, evidence, or approved language.
6. Uniform rhythm, repeated openings, excessive headings or lists, repeated trigrams, and suspicious vocabulary density.
7. Complexity without added precision.

Revise only real defects. The preferred repair order is: return to the source claim, restore concrete evidence, reuse approved language where suitable, simplify the sentence, then restructure if needed. Do not solve generic writing by swapping in fancier synonyms.

For idea-led online writing, add a CRIBS pass:
- confusing;
- repetitive;
- interesting;
- boring;
- surprising.

Treat these as reader reactions, not mechanical scores. Interesting and surprising sections may deserve more space. Confusing or boring sections may reveal a weak idea rather than a weak sentence.

Before flagging a marked device, run the form-match test. Long sentences, repetition, rhetorical questions, symmetry, metaphor, fragments, and unusual structure are defects only when they fail to earn their effect. Do not flatten intentional prose into uniform short sentences.

Run a second audit. Stop when remaining flags are legitimate, quoted, technical, source-faithful, voice-authentic, functionally earned, or mere preference.
