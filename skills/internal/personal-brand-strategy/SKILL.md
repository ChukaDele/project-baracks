# Personal Brand Strategy

Find a useful idea the named person can honestly own. Start from supplied experiences, decisions, disagreements, observations, questions, and approved voice samples.

Separate three things before drafting:
1. Commodity knowledge the audience could get anywhere.
2. The person's chosen position, experience, or interpretation.
3. The genuinely new question or distinction worth developing further.

Do not turn commodity knowledge into fake originality through stronger adjectives or punchier formatting. If the personal layer is missing, expose the gap or write a neutral useful piece. Never invent anecdotes, feelings, credentials, motives, or first-person history.

Use research to pressure-test the idea. Look for counterexamples and boundary conditions before sharpening the claim. Prose Craft writes the final piece. Public or consequential work requires independent red-team review.
